Star rendering and star-coloured lighting: design notes
========================================================

THESE NOTES ARE AI-GENERATED AND PLACED IN THE PUBLIC DOMAIN, recorded for
review.  They capture design decisions and deferred ideas from the shadow-
mapping / sun-rendering work so the choices (and the roads not taken) are not
lost.  Nothing here is binding; it is a record for a future decision.

Context
-------
The renderer has no HDR framebuffer, no bloom post-pass, and no auto-exposure.
It tonemaps PER SHADER with a filmic (Uncharted-2) curve (filmic_tonemap in
graph_dev_opengl.c, gain ~1.18) and writes to an 8-bit buffer.  That curve
asymptotes to ~1.18 per channel, so a bright, saturated colour driven through it
desaturates toward white on its own.  This single fact shapes the sun design.


1. The sun ("MATERIAL_SUN"): procedural emission through the shared tonemap
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A camera-facing billboard drawn with a dedicated shader (share/snis/shader/
sun.{vert,frag}).  The whole star is one EMISSION field, not stacked layers:

    emission = blackbody(T) * (core_brightness * coreProfile(r)
                               + bloom_brightness * bloomProfile(r))
    f_FragColor = filmic_tonemap(vec4(emission, coverage))

  - coreProfile: ~flat inside the disc, soft edge at the disc radius.
  - bloomProfile: from the disc EDGE outward (not the centre, or it hides behind
    the growing disc), pow(1 - t, bloom_gamma).
  - coverage (alpha): the core is opaque (hides background); the bloom is additive
    (adds light).  Premultiplied-alpha blend (GL_ONE, GL_ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA).

Why this shape:
  - Feeding HDR-range emission through the SAME filmic tonemap the rest of the
    scene uses makes the white-hot core fall out for free and stay consistent
    with everything else: crank core_brightness and the centre clips to white
    while the limb keeps the star's colour.  No separate "white layer" needed.
  - The disc size is always the physical star size (2 * star radius, world-scale);
    only the bloom is screen-scale.

Star colour from temperature (blackbody):
  - Colour is a blackbody chromaticity from a Kelvin temperature (~2500K red
    dwarf .. ~30000K O/B blue), not discrete presets.  A cheap Kelvin->RGB
    approximation (Tanner Helland) is enough.  Caveats: the blackbody locus goes
    red -> dark, never magenta ("dim magenta" for sub-stellar bodies is artistic
    licence); a star actually lighting a system realistically stays within the
    ~2500-30000K band.

Controls (tuned in shadow_lab, then baked as defaults): temperature, core
brightness, disc edge softness, bloom screen width, bloom gamma/falloff, bloom
brightness.

Promotion to the game: swap the game's sun_material to material_init_sun with the
disc colour from each solar system's sun colour (or a per-system temperature), and
add a GLES fallback (route MATERIAL_SUN to the existing textured billboard on the
Pi, which has no case for it yet).  The blackbody Kelvin->RGB helper should move
from shadow_lab to a shared util at that point.


2. Decision: procedural sun now vs. true HDR + screen-space bloom later
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
True HDR (render to an FP16 buffer, extract/blur/composite bloom, one final
tonemap, optional luminance-based auto-exposure) is the architecturally correct,
better-looking answer:
  - one bloom system for ALL bright sources (sun, thrusters, weapons, explosions,
    specular, bright stars), correctly screen-space at any distance/angle;
  - real auto-exposure (dim everything else when the sun is in view) for free;
  - and the one qualitative win procedural cannot match: the sun's glow spills
    over objects IN FRONT of it (a ship silhouetted against the sun, light past a
    station's edge), because the bloom lives in the image, not on the sun's quad.

But in THIS codebase it is a renderer refactor with wide blast radius:
  - filmic_tonemap must be removed from ~20 lit shaders across both backends and
    both shader dirs (otherwise values are pre-compressed and there is no HDR
    headroom for bloom);
  - an FP16 target has to survive the two-pass depth-boundary split in
    render_entities(), the CSM pre-pass, and the blended billboards;
  - new passes (bright-extract, down/up-sample, blur, composite, tonemap);
  - GLES/RPi fill-rate cost (likely desktop-only, i.e. another twin path);
  - it re-tonemaps the ENTIRE game, so it is the project owner's aesthetic call.

Decision: ship the procedural sun now.  It is isolated, previewable, tunable, and
FORWARD-COMPATIBLE -- its output is just blackbody(T) * intensity, so under a
future HDR pipeline you delete the per-object bloom and per-object tonemap and let
the star emit those same high intensities into the HDR buffer.  The per-object
"emission -> filmic_tonemap" is deliberately a per-object mini-HDR: the stepping
stone.  Do not gate the sun (or the shadow branch) on the HDR refactor; pitch HDR
separately if the whole-scene payoff is judged worth the cost.


3. Follow-up: star-coloured diffuse light and inverse-tinted ambient
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Tint the direct (diffuse) light SLIGHTLY toward the star's colour, and tint the
ambient / shaded term SLIGHTLY toward the opposite (complement): a red star ->
faintly red sunlight and faintly cyan shadow; a blue star -> faintly blue light
and faintly warm shadow.  This is the classic warm-light / cool-shadow art
technique and it reads as far richer for very little cost.

Honesty check: in atmosphere this is physical (sunlight vs. blue-sky fill); in
vacuum there is no sky, so the cool ambient is pure stylisation -- fine, but do not
over-apply (a few percent) or it looks cartoonish.  Mechanism: derive the two
tints from the star's blackbody colour (ambient tint = a desaturated complement),
multiply them into the existing diffuse and ambient terms in the lit shaders (the
lighting is already there; this is a small tint, not new lighting).  Best done
after the sun itself looks right; test in shadow_lab against the temperature slider.


4. Follow-up (capture only; highest risk): fake HDR via sun screen coverage
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: reduce the brightness of everything except the sun based on how much of the
screen the sun billboard covers (small weight for the bloom region, large for the
core), to fake the sun feeling brighter than the display's maximum.

This is really eye/camera AUTO-EXPOSURE.  Risks: it is global (needs an exposure
multiplier threaded through every lit shader before tonemap -- invasive); screen
coverage is a crude proxy (real auto-exposure integrates scene luminance); and
without temporal adaptation it will PULSE as the sun enters/leaves view or the
camera turns.  Bloom + white-hot core already buy most of the "blinding" feel for
a fraction of the risk.  Recommendation: park it; if ever done, do it as proper
luminance-based auto-exposure on an HDR buffer (see section 2), not as a
sun-coverage hack.
